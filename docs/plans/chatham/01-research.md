# Chatham House Rule mode: research brief (01)

Status: research input for design. Compiled 18 Oct 2026 from five parallel research passes using web_search/web_fetch.
Convention: every claim carries the URL that was actually fetched. "(unverified)" means no primary page confirmed it. Several sources are dated 2026, so recheck them before shipping.

> **Headline finding.** Chatham House revised its official Rule page around 16 Oct 2026. It now answers **"Can I use an AI notetaker/transcription tool during an 'under the Rule' meeting? No."** and **"Can I record an 'under the Rule' meeting? No."** ([live page via reader](https://r.jina.ai/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule)). A design that captions or transcribes a session described as "under the Chatham House Rule" therefore contradicts the Rule's custodian. The centenary framing has to be built around this (see §C).

## Part A. The Rule itself

*Access note: chathamhouse.org returns HTTP 403 (Cloudflare) to direct fetches. The live page was read through the r.jina.ai reader, and older versions through Wayback ([2023](https://web.archive.org/web/20231229180643id_/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule), [2018](https://web.archive.org/web/20180310181155id_/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about/chatham-house-rule)). The canonical URL is https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule.*

### A1. Wording, singular vs plural
- **Official wording (verbatim):** "When a meeting, or part thereof, is held under the Chatham House Rule, participants are free to use the information received, but neither the identity nor the affiliation of the speaker(s), nor that of any other participant, may be revealed." ([live](https://r.jina.ai/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule)). The wording is identical in the 2018 and 2023 captures ([2018](https://web.archive.org/web/20180310181155id_/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about/chatham-house-rule), [2023](https://web.archive.org/web/20231229180643id_/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule)).
- **Singular, not plural:** the FAQ "Should one refer to the Chatham House Rule or the Chatham House Rules?" is answered "There is only one Rule." ([live](https://r.jina.ai/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule)). The text's own "speaker(s)" is the only plural form it contains. "Chatham House Rules" is a misnomer ([ABC Media Watch 2012](https://www.abc.net.au/mediawatch/episodes/recording-mr-jones/9973906)).
- **Language:** "The definitive Chatham House Rule was formulated in the English language." Official translations exist, including Chinese, and Chatham House disclaims responsibility for errors in them ([live](https://r.jina.ai/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule)). Lares should display the official Chinese translation verbatim, not its own rendering.

### A2. Origin and refinements
- **Devised:** "In 1927 and refined in 1992 and 2002." ([live](https://r.jina.ai/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule); same in [2018](https://web.archive.org/web/20180310181155id_/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about/chatham-house-rule)).
- **Our History timeline, 1927:** "The Rule is formally adopted, encouraging a trusted environment designed to understand and resolve complex problems." ([Our history](https://r.jina.ai/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/our-history)).
- **Month:** "June 1927" (and "October 1992") appears only on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_House_Rule), whose source is the Chatham House page, and that page gives years only. **The month is unverified.** Do not put "June 1927" in marketing without a primary source.
- **Original 1927 text and the 1992/2002 changes:** **unverified.** No primary text was found; Chatham House publishes only the current wording.

### A3. What it permits and forbids; the spirit
- **Permits** use of "the information received". **Forbids** revealing the "identity nor the affiliation of the speaker(s), nor that of any other participant" ([live](https://r.jina.ai/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule)). Every attendee is protected, not only speakers.
- **Scope:** it applies to "a meeting, or part thereof". However, "a host or speakers cannot switch back and forth, declaring some remarks to be under the Rule and others not" ([live](https://r.jina.ai/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule)).
- **Open to anyone:** "Any group of people can use the Rule as a pre-agreed guide for running an event." ([live](https://r.jina.ai/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule)).
- **Purpose:** "It allows people to speak as individuals, and to express views that may not be those of their organizations… It encourages free discussion and the assessing of ideas free of the affiliations of those who contributed them." ([live](https://r.jina.ai/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule)).
- **Spirit:** "The Rule is more about the dissemination of the information after the event – nothing should be done to identify, either explicitly or implicitly, who said what." ([2023](https://web.archive.org/web/20231229180643id_/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule)).
- **Not the strictest regime:** "In cases where the Rule is not considered sufficiently strict, an event may be held 'off the record'." ([2023](https://web.archive.org/web/20231229180643id_/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule)). Chatham House's own events are normally "on the record", "with the Rule only occasionally invoked, usually for smaller groups" ([live](https://r.jina.ai/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule)).
- **Must be explicit:** "It should be clearly stated at the start of a meeting if it is to be held under the Chatham House rule. It should never be implied or assumed." (Chatham House press officer, quoted by [ABC Media Watch](https://www.abc.net.au/mediawatch/episodes/recording-mr-jones/9973906)).

### A7. Misunderstandings and misuses
- **Carrie Lam, 2019:** audio of a private lunch was leaked. She said the session "clearly was subject to Chatham House Rule had been recorded and then passed to the media" ([news.gov.hk](https://www.news.gov.hk/eng/2019/09/20190903/20190903_101944_851.html); [ABC 中文](https://newsapp.abc.net.au/chinese/2019-09-03/what-is-chatham-house-rule/11475404)). Lesson: a recording turns the Rule into a liability, because the voice itself identifies the speaker.
- **Alan Jones, 2012:** he claimed "there was Chatham House rules" after a covert recording surfaced. Media Watch found no evidence the Rule was announced ("we reckon it didn't happen"). The reporter said "we probably would have published anyway" ([Media Watch](https://www.abc.net.au/mediawatch/episodes/recording-mr-jones/9973906)). The case shows three failures: the plural misnomer, the Rule asserted after the fact, and deliberate defiance.
- **ECB / Cœuré, 2015:** remarks at a hedge-fund dinner moved markets, and the EU Ombudsman demanded an account ([RTÉ](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2015/0528/704482-eu-ecb-ombudsman/)). Wikipedia says the dinner was under the Rule; RTÉ does not say so (unverified). Lesson: the Rule frees the content, so sensitive information still travels.
- **Bilderberg:** describes the Rule as "participants agree not to quote each other", and publishes its full participant list ([Bilderberg FAQ](https://www.bilderbergmeetings.org/frequently-asked-questions)). Both differ from Chatham House's own wording and 2023 FAQ.
- **IETF/IAB RFC 9969 (2026):** a workshop report has a "Chatham House Rule" section yet publishes the attendee list ([RFC 9969](https://ftp.nic.ad.jp/in-notes/authors/rfc9969-auth48diff.html)).
- **"Chatham House = off the record":** a common misreading. The text says the opposite ("free to use the information"), and "off the record" is a separate, stricter status ([2023](https://web.archive.org/web/20231229180643id_/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule)). No specific cited instance was fetched.

### B2. On-device small LLMs (summarisation, de-identification)

**Apple Foundation Models (OS 26+)**
- **Model:** ~3B parameters, 2-bit QAT, 15 languages ([Apple ML](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/apple-foundation-models-2025-updates)), including Chinese Simplified and Traditional ([apple.com](https://www.apple.com/apple-intelligence/)).
- **Context:** 4,096 tokens per session ([TN3193](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/Technotes/tn3193-managing-the-on-device-foundation-model-s-context-window)), so transcripts must be chunked.
- **Devices:** iPhone 15 Pro or later, M1 or later. Check `SystemLanguageModel.availability` at runtime ([doc](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/foundationmodels/systemlanguagemodel.json)).
- **Mainland China:** "will not currently work for supported devices purchased in China mainland" ([support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/121115)). A trial was reported in Oct 2026 ([china.org.cn](http://spanish.china.org.cn/txt/2026-10/16/content_118736387.htm)); developer API availability there is unverified.
- **New at WWDC26:** the same framework can call a PCC server model with 32K context. No token fees. Limited to apps with <2M downloads, by application ([WWDC26-319](https://developer.apple.com/cn/videos/play/wwdc2026/319/)).

**Gemini Nano / ML Kit GenAI**
- **Summarization API:** under 4,000 tokens of input; accepts `CONVERSATION` input; **English, Korean and Japanese only, no Chinese**; blocked on unlocked bootloaders ([ML Kit](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai/summarization/android?hl=fr)).
- **Devices:** a fixed flagship list ([overview](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai?hl=en)).
- **Prompt API:** supports structured output ([Prompt API](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai/prompt/android?hl=en)); Chinese support unverified.
- **China:** depends on Google Play services, so likely absent on mainland devices (unverified).

**Qwen3 (Apache 2.0)**
- **Sizes:** 0.6B / 1.7B / 4B, all with 32K context, 119 languages ([Qwen blog](https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen3/)).
- **Q4_K_M files:** 397 MB / 1.11 GB / 2.5 GB ([0.6B](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3-0.6B-GGUF/tree/main), [1.7B](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3-1.7B-GGUF/tree/main), [4B](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3-4B-GGUF/tree/main)).
- **Speed:** on a Snapdragon 860 CPU with llama.cpp, 0.6B Q8 ran at 16.2 tok/s and 1.7B Q8 at 8.2 tok/s ([juejin](https://juejin.cn/post/7506727402821042191)). Alibaba's MNN claims 2.3× faster decode than llama.cpp ([MNN](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alibaba/MNN/master/apps/Android/MnnLlmChat/README.md)).

**Phi-4-mini**
- 128K context, MIT licence, Chinese among its 24 languages. Its card admits "multilingual performance and safety gaps" ([HF](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Phi-4-mini-instruct)). Not recommended for Chinese.

**Flutter integration**
- `flutter_gemma` has been renamed `flutter_edge_ai` 2.4.0. It supports LiteRT-LM and ONNX, and can route to OS models (Gemini Nano, Apple FM, Windows AI Foundry) ([pub.dev](https://pub.dev/packages/flutter_edge_ai)).
- `llama_cpp_dart` runs GGUF models on all four OSes ([pub.dev](https://pub.dev/packages/llama_cpp_dart)).

**Limits for redaction**
- LLM redactors leak PII to a degree that depends on architecture and fine-tuning ([PRvL, arXiv 2508.05545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05545)).
- Small models miss spans and may rewrite facts.
- Use the LLM as a constrained span-tagger (structured output), not as a free rewriter, and gate it behind rules and NER.

**Recommendation:**
- Qwen3-1.7B (phones) or Qwen3-4B (desktop) is the only option that is cross-platform, strong in Chinese and usable in mainland China.
- Treat Apple FM and Gemini Nano as opportunistic accelerators.

### B3. De-identification and PII redaction

**Presidio**
- **Structure:** an Analyzer (recognizers, spaCy/Stanza/transformers NLP engine, context enhancer) and an Anonymizer. Operators: `replace`, `redact`, `hash` (random salt per entity by default since v2.2.361), `mask`, `encrypt`/`decrypt`, `custom`, `keep` ([Anonymizer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/data-privacy-stack/presidio/main/docs/anonymizer/index.md), [Analyzer](https://presidio.dataprivacystack.org/analyzer/)).
- **No stateful pseudonymisation:** "does not store or maintain stateful sessions" ([Anonymizer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/data-privacy-stack/presidio/main/docs/anonymizer/index.md)).
- **English only by default:** Chinese needs a zh NLP model plus custom recognizers and context words ([languages](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/data-privacy-stack/presidio/main/docs/analyzer/languages.md)).
- **Weak on context:** span tools "mainly remove structured information" ([Staab et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.13846v2)).

**Chinese NER**

| Model | Training data | Score |
|---|---|---|
| spaCy `zh_core_web_trf` 3.7.2 | OntoNotes, 18 labels | P/R/F 76.09 / 72.18 / 74.08 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/spacy/zh_core_web_trf)) |
| spaCy `zh_core_web_lg` | — | F 71.34 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/spacy/zh_core_web_lg/raw/main/README.md)) |
| HanLP `MSRA_NER_ELECTRA_SMALL_ZH` | MSRA newswire | F1 95.16 ([HanLP](https://hanlp.hankcs.com/docs/api/hanlp/pretrained/ner.html)) |
| LTP 4 | — | Rust NER, 3.55× faster than v3 ([GitHub](https://github.com/HIT-SCIR/ltp)) |

Spoken, code-switched transcripts will score lower (unverified). At about 72% recall, NER cannot be the only safeguard.

**Attribute and attribution inference**
- **Staab, Vero, Balunović, Vechev, *Beyond Memorization* (ICLR 2024):**
  - Data: PersonalReddit, 520 profiles and 5,814 comments.
  - GPT-4 inferred personal attributes with ~85% top-1 and 95.2% top-3 accuracy, at 100× lower cost and 240× less time than humans.
  - Inference survives Azure anonymisation ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07298), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07298v2)).
- **Staab et al., *LLMs are Advanced Anonymizers* (arXiv 2402.13846; venue year unverified):**
  - Method: an adversary LLM infers attributes and explains its cues, then an anonymizer LLM generalises or removes those cues, over several rounds.
  - Results: Azure only lowered adversary accuracy from 86.7% to 66.3%; three adversarial rounds reached 45.3%.
  - Humans (n=50) preferred the LLM-anonymised text ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13846), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.13846v2)).
  - Related: SynthPAI ([2406.07217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07217)) and RUPTA ([2407.11770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11770)).
- **Stylometry:**
  - LLMs attribute authorship zero-shot among 10–20 candidates (Huang, Chen, Shu, EMNLP 2024 Findings, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08213)). That is roughly Lares' circle size.
  - On interview transcripts, style paraphrase cut identification from 70% to 24% (Sinha et al., SPSC 2024, [ISCA](https://www.isca-archive.org/spsc_2024/sinha24_spsc.html)).
- **Agentic re-identification:**
  - LLM agents with web search linked 6 of 24 scientist interviews in Anthropic's public Interviewer dataset to specific published works, sometimes identifying the person uniquely.
  - Splitting the attack into benign-looking subtasks got around safeguards (Li 2026, [arXiv 2601.05918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05918)).
  - This is the closest published analogue to the Lares threat.
- **TAB (Pilán, Lison, Øvrelid, Papadopoulou, Sánchez, Batet 2022):**
  - Corpus: 1,268 ECHR cases annotated for direct and quasi identifiers, co-reference and masking.
  - Metrics: ER_di and ER_qi (an entity counts as protected only if *all* its mentions are masked), and WP_di+qi (information-weighted precision, a utility measure) ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00443), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.00443v2)).
- **The small-group k problem:**
  - k-anonymity means each person is indistinguishable from at least k−1 others ([Sweeney 2002](https://dataprivacylab.org/dataprivacy/projects/kanonymity/kanonymity.html)).
  - In a circle of n = 4–8, k ≤ n, and any unique role gives k = 1.
  - Insiders know the roster, so de-identification protects only against outsiders (inference).

### B4. Ephemeral and "no-trace" processing

**Apple PCC**
- **Requirements:** "Stateless computation on personal user data. Enforceable guarantees. No privileged runtime access. Non-targetability. Verifiable transparency." ([Apple SEAR 2024](https://security.apple.com/blog/private-cloud-compute/)).
- **Stateless:** "no user data is retained in any form after the response is returned."
- **Transparency log:** measurements of all code are published in an append-only log.
- **Research access:** the VRE and the Security Bounty were added Oct 2024 ([SEAR](https://security.apple.com/blog/pcc-security-research/)).
- **Google Cloud extension (Jun 2026):** runs on NVIDIA CC + Intel TDX + Titan with "at least two separate roots of trust" ([SEAR](https://security.apple.com/blog/expanding-pcc)).
- **Third-party access:** now available via Foundation Models, for apps under 2M downloads ([WWDC26-319](https://developer.apple.com/cn/videos/play/wwdc2026/319/)).

**NVIDIA H100 confidential computing**
- **Protection chain:** measured boot, SPDM session to a CPU TEE, signed attestation report, encrypted bounce buffers, encrypted and signed kernels ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/confidential-computing-on-h100-gpus-for-secure-and-trustworthy-ai/)).
- **Cost:** compute and HBM run at parity, but CPU↔GPU transfer drops to ~4 GB/s.
- **NRAS:** issues signed EAT tokens, and NVIDIA collects IP addresses and GPU information ([NRAS](https://docs.nvidia.com/attestation/cloud-services/latest/nras/nras_introduction.html)).
- Blackwell specifics are unverified.

**Clouds and services**
- **Azure NCCadsH100v5:** SEV-SNP plus H100; the TEE spans CPU and GPU ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/confidential-computing/gpu-options)).
- **Azure confidential inferencing:** offered for Azure OpenAI Whisper, with OHTTP ([MS Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/confidential-computing/overview-azure-products)); the gateway is open source ([GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/attested-ohttp-server)).
- **GCP:** confidential GPU VMs on A3 and G4 ([GCP](https://docs.cloud.google.com/confidential-computing/confidential-vm/docs/create-a-confidential-vm-instance-with-gpu)).
- **Tinfoil:** client-verified enclaves running vLLM, with Sigstore transparency ([docs](https://docs.tinfoil.sh/verification/verification-in-tinfoil.md)).
- **Phala:** an attested gateway with signed receipts ([docs](https://docs.phala.com/phala-cloud/confidential-ai/overview)).
- **WhatsApp Private Processing:** OHTTP relay, RA-TLS, a third-party ledger, and "Stateless processing and forward security" ([Meta](https://engineering.fb.com/2025/04/29/security/whatsapp-private-processing-ai-tools/)).
- **Confer (Marlinspike):** Noise handshake bound to an attestation quote, dm-verity, reproducible builds, a transparency log; "The host never sees plaintext" ([Confer](https://confer.to/blog/2026/01/private-inference/)). Reported to underpin Meta AI ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/signals-creator-is-helping-encrypt-meta-ai/)).

**Remote attestation**
- RFC 9334 defines the roles: Attester → Evidence → Verifier (policy, reference values) → Attestation Result → Relying Party, with freshness ([RFC 9334](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9334.html)).
- **Limits:**
  - Vendors exclude physical attacks; TEE.fail (Oct 2025) broke DDR5 TEEs with a cheap interposer ([Ars](https://arstechnica.com/security/2025/10/new-physical-attacks-are-quickly-diluting-secure-enclave-defenses-from-nvidia-amd-and-intel/)).
  - Attestation proves *which* code runs, not that the code behaves well.

### B5. Differential privacy and k-anonymity for summaries
- **DP-Prompt (Utpala, Hooker, Chen, EMNLP 2023 Findings):** LLM paraphrase as local DP. It kept sentiment F1 while cutting author-identification F1 by 46% (static attacker) and 26% (adaptive attacker) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16111)).
- **Metric DP on embeddings (Feyisetan et al.):** <2% utility loss on classifiers ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08902)).
- **Word-level DP is weak:** poor guarantees and output (Mattern et al., NAACL 2022 Findings, [ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2022.findings-naacl.65/)).
- **"DP" claims fail in practice:** ADePT was shown not to be DP (Habernal, EMNLP 2021, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03175)).
- **Why DP fits poorly here (inference):**
  - DP protects membership in aggregates.
  - A single meeting summary is one document whose purpose is to reveal specific content.
  - Noise strong enough to hide who said a distinctive claim also destroys the claim.
  - DP-style paraphrase is therefore useful only as a heuristic style obfuscator. Do not claim DP.
- **k / l-diversity analogue (inference):**
  - Attribute a claim to "a participant" only if ≥k attendees could plausibly have said it.
  - Group labels ("one of the engineers") must not have uniform stances, or the label leaks the stance.
  - The l-diversity primary source was not fetched (unverified).
- **Gap:** no peer-reviewed work on speaker de-attribution in meeting summaries was found. Nearest: Lison et al. 2021 ([ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.323/)).

### B6. P2P data channels, WebRTC and LiveKit

**WebRTC data channels**
- Transport: SCTP/DTLS/UDP, with reliable and partially reliable modes ([RFC 8831](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8831.html)).
- Size: default max-message-size is 64 KB when it isn't in the SDP; browsers send at least 256 KB ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebRTC_API/Using_data_channels)).
- Path: true P2P traffic "never passes through the web or application server".

**LiveKit `publishData`**
- **Delivery:** everything goes via the SFU. Reliable mode is ordered and retransmitted but best-effort and not buffered; up to 15 KiB payload. Lossy mode should stay ≤1300 B ([packets](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/data/packets.md)).
- **Text streams:** auto-chunked, supported in Flutter, no history kept ([text streams](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/data/text-streams.md)).

**LiveKit E2EE**
- **Coverage:** media plus data packets and streams, but **only via the new `RoomOptions.encryption` field**. The deprecated `e2ee` field does not encrypt data. Signalling is not E2EE ([encryption](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/encryption.md)).
- **Keys are the app's job:** LiveKit "does not (and cannot) store or transport encryption keys".
- **Flutter:** `BaseKeyProvider` plus a custom provider for MLS or rotation ([start](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/encryption/start.md)).
- **Critical:** an AI agent is "just another participant" and must hold the key to decrypt speech ([agents](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/encryption/agents.md)). E2EE does not hide content from the AI or its vendors.

**Mesh**
- A full mesh needs n(n−1)/2 links ([Stream](https://getstream.io/blog/building-a-conferencing-app/)). LiveKit says P2P media suits 2–3 peers ([SFU internals](https://docs.livekit.io/reference/internals/livekit-sfu.md)).
- A data-only mesh is cheaper but still needs one DTLS connection per pair (inference).
- SFU plus E2EE gives similar confidentiality with less complexity.

### B7. Cryptographic primitives

**MLS (RFC 9420, Jul 2023)**
- Asynchronous group keys with forward secrecy and post-compromise security, for groups "from two to thousands". Each Commit starts a new epoch ([RFC 9420](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9420.html)).
- **Libraries:**
  - mls-rs: 100% conformance, not third-party audited ([mls-rs](https://awslabs.github.io/mls-rs/)).
  - OpenMLS: mobile targets build in CI but are untested ([docs](https://latest.openmls.tech/doc/openmls/)).
  - Dart `openmls` 3.2.1, via flutter_rust_bridge; community package ([pub.dev](https://pub.dev/packages/openmls)).

**MLS in products**
- **Discord DAVE:**
  - The voice gateway acts as the MLS delivery service.
  - Per-sender ratcheted frame keys are applied via encoded transforms.
  - A new epoch starts on every membership change.
  - Handshake messages are not E2EE (metadata).
  - Enforced for official clients in 2025 ([DAVE](https://daveprotocol.com/)).
- **Webex:** MLS plus SFrame (RFC 9605). Standard meetings are hop-by-hop so the cloud can transcribe ([Webex](https://help.webex.com/en-us/article/nhxkyce/Webex-Suite-Meetings-Security-Technical-Paper)).
- **RCS:** Universal Profile 3.0 E2EE is MLS-based ([GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/article/rcs-encryption-a-leap-towards-secure-and-interoperable-messaging/)); iPhone↔Android beta started May 2026 ([Infosecurity](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/apple-google-rcs-end-to-end/)).

**Signal**
- **Sealed sender:** sender certificates plus 96-bit delivery tokens hide the sender from the server ([Signal](https://signal.org/blog/sealed-sender/)).
- **Double Ratchet:** deletable per-message keys and break-in recovery. Storage recovery is "outside the scope" ([spec](https://signal.org/docs/specifications/doubleratchet/)).

**Threshold release**
- **FROST (RFC 9591, Jun 2024, Informational):** t-of-n Schnorr signatures in two rounds. **Not robust:** one bad signer can block signing, though the culprit can be identified ([RFC 9591](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9591.html)).
- **Shamir secret sharing:** k-of-n secret reconstruction. The secret exists in full when split and when reconstructed, and plain Shamir is not verifiable ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamir%27s_secret_sharing)).
- No production Dart threshold-encryption library was verified.

**Verifiable deletion**
- Proofs of Secure Erasure need bounded-memory devices with ROM and are "not cheap" ([ePrint 2010/217](https://eprint.iacr.org/2010/217)).
- Cryptographic erase is recognised by NIST SP 800-88r2 ([NIST](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/88/r2/final)).
- Crypto-shredding can't reach plaintext copies such as screenshots or vendor logs. Remote deletion is unprovable without attestation (inference).

## Part C. Synthesis: implications for the Lares design

**C1. Reconcile with the Oct 2026 guidance (the decision with the most consequences).** Chatham House now says no to AI notetakers and no to recording in Rule meetings ([live](https://r.jina.ai/https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/chatham-house-rule)). Options:
- **(a) "Under the Rule" means the AI goes dark.** Captions and the AI are disabled for the session, and the tribute is the protocol (explicit invocation, session scope, participant protection).
- **(b) A mode "inspired by" the Rule,** not "under" it. Ephemeral captions with no notes, and outward output only as de-identified, participant-approved summaries. Never claim the session is "held under the Chatham House Rule".
- **(c) Ask Chatham House directly** for its position ahead of the centenary.

Avoid "Chatham House Rules" (plural) and unverified "June 1927" in copy.

**C2. Protocol fidelity:**
- Declare the mode explicitly at the start ("never implied or assumed").
- Scope it per session, with no per-remark toggling.
- Protect *all* participants: no attendee list goes outward.
- Each person may export only their own words (self-attribution is allowed).

**C3. "No trace" must be honest:**
- **The agent holds the room key under LiveKit E2EE.** "No trace" is a property of processing location plus retention, not of cryptography.
- **Strongest claim available:** on-device ASR and LLM, held in memory only, with crypto-shredding of session keys at the end.
- **Global users:** for server inference, use attested TEEs (Tinfoil, Azure/GCP CC, PCC on iOS) with client-side verification and OHTTP.
- **Mainland China users:** none of the fetched sources offers verifiable ephemerality there, so on-device processing is the only honest path.
- **Wording:** never say "leaves no trace". Say "not stored and not readable by us".

**C4. Outward publication pipeline:**
1. Deterministic roster deny-list (names, aliases, organisations).
2. zh/en NER (HanLP / spaCy trf in Presidio with custom recognizers).
3. LLM span-tagging with structured output.
4. Neutral-voice paraphrase (stylometry).
5. Generalise roles; drop unique and web-searchable facts.
6. No per-speaker pseudonyms; use theme lists.
7. An adversarial attacker LLM is given the roster and must be held to chance (~1/n) or abstain ([Staab 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.13846v2), [Li 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05918)).
8. Each speaker can veto, and k-of-n participants approve via FROST-signed release ([RFC 9591](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9591.html)), designed for a stalled signer.

**C5. State the threat model plainly:** de-identification protects against *outside* readers, not against fellow participants (k ≤ n). Do not claim DP.

**C6. Transport:**
- LiveKit `encryption` (not `e2ee`) so captions and AI text are E2EE data streams.
- MLS key management, one epoch per membership change, following DAVE.
- The AI agent is a visible MLS member, shown with a persistent indicator plus an audible announcement (Zoom/Apple pattern).
- Every participant opts in affirmatively (the Otter lesson).
- Private by default (the Granola lesson).
- Pseudonymise signalling metadata.

**C7. ASR/LLM stack:**
- sherpa-onnx bilingual streaming everywhere, plus SpeechTranscriber on Apple 26+.
- Qwen3-1.7B/4B via llama.cpp or MNN as the portable LLM; Apple FM or Gemini Nano opportunistically.
- Note that Gemini's Summarization API lacks Chinese, and Apple FM is unavailable in mainland China today.
